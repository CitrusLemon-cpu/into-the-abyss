Reference 08 · Human diving

Freediver · 253 m

Deepest no-limit freedive (male)

Guinness World Records · Accessed 9 September 2026

Herbert Nitsch reached 253 metres in Santorini, Greece, on 6 June 2012. This is the no-limit discipline, which allows assistance for descent and ascent, rather than a swimming-only record. The illustration marks the Guinness-listed depth.

Reference 09 · Human diving

Scuba diver · 332.35 m

Deepest scuba dive (male)

Guinness World Records · Accessed 9 September 2026

Ahmed Gabr reached 332.35 metres off Dahab, Egypt, in the Red Sea on 18 September 2014. The illustration marks the record depth; it does not depict the equipment used on the actual dive.

Reference 01

Portuguese man-o'-war

What is a Portuguese Man o’ War?

NOAA National Ocean Service · Accessed 9 September 2026

A Portuguese man-o’-war is a colony of specialised animals working together. Its gas-filled float stays at the surface, while tentacles trail below. Winds and ocean currents carry the colony through tropical and subtropical seas.

Reference 02

Manatee

Florida Manatee

U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service · Accessed 9 September 2026

The Florida manatee, a subspecies of the West Indian manatee shown here, grazes on seagrasses and other aquatic plants. Its molars are replaced throughout its life as abrasive food wears them down. In cooler months, Florida manatees gather at warm-water refuges such as natural springs.

Reference 03

Sea otter

Southern Sea Otter

Marine Mammal Commission · Accessed 9 September 2026

Sea otters have the densest fur of any animal, with roughly 250,000 to one million hairs per square inch. This thick coat helps insulate them in cold water. The southern sea otter is the subspecies found along the California coast.

Reference 04

Atlantic puffin

Atlantic Puffin — Field Guide

National Audubon Society · Accessed 9 September 2026

Atlantic puffins hunt by swimming underwater and can return to their nests carrying several small fish in their bills at once. They nest around the North Atlantic. Outside the breeding season, they spend the winter far from land on the open ocean.

Reference 05

Mahi-mahi

Pacific Mahimahi

NOAA Fisheries · Accessed 9 September 2026

Mahi-mahi are among the ocean’s fastest-growing fish: they can exceed 1.2 metres in their first year. Also called dolphinfish or dorado, they hunt near the surface during the day, feeding on small fish and invertebrates. They can begin reproducing at just four to five months old.

Reference 06

Phytoplankton

What are plankton?

NOAA National Ocean Service · Accessed 9 September 2026

Phytoplankton are tiny drifting organisms that use sunlight to make food through photosynthesis, taking in carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen. They live near the sunlit surface and help support marine food webs. Phytoplankton is a group of organisms, rather than a single species.

Reference 07

Green sea turtle

Green Turtle

NOAA Fisheries · Accessed 9 September 2026

Young green turtles spend part of their lives in the open ocean, feeding on plant and animal life in floating communities such as Sargassum. Later, they move into nearshore feeding areas. Their diet and habitat change as they grow.
